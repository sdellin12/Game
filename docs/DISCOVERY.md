## Consolidated queue
Q01. Describe the ideal moment in a match: what happens that makes your family laugh, cheer, or say 'that was awesome'?
Q02. What ages and reading abilities must independent play support, and what help should younger players receive?
Q03. What player count should a typical match support?
Q04. How long should a typical match take?
Q05. Which parts of Commander should carry over, and which should disappear? Clarify leader identity, singleton/deck size, politics, elimination, long turns and complex responses individually as needed.
Q06. Should players fight each other, compete against shared threats, or have multiple modes?
Q07. How should a player win?
Q08. What happens to a player who is defeated or falls far behind?
Q09. What forms of helping and betrayal should the game allow? Clarify whether promises are enforced, trading/lending, rewards, targeting and frustrating interference.
Q10. Should themes change appearance/story, actual mechanics, or both?
Q11. What should make a themed deck feel personally yours?
Q12. Should deck creation select existing cards, customize approved templates, invent abilities, use suggestions from a description, or combine these? Do not assume AI.
Q13. How should custom cards be balanced and approved? Clarify whether all effects must execute automatically.
Q14. Should decks have a central leader, and what role should it play?
Q15. What should a typical turn involve?
Q16. When and how should opponents respond during another player's turn?
Q17. How should resources and card costs work?
Q18. How should combat and battlefield positioning work?
Q19. How should powerful combos, reversals and disruptive effects work?
Q20. How much randomness beyond card draws is desirable?
Q21. Should younger/newer players have optional help, simpler decks or balancing assistance?
Q22. Should cards be shared, unlocked, earned or created over time? Clarify power versus strategy variety and personal collections.
Q23. How much initial learning and deck-building is enjoyable before playing?
Q24. What content and art preferences or limits apply? Clarify recognizable franchise characters versus original analogues and eventual distribution intent.
Q25. Is the intended audience household, invited friends or eventual public release?
Q26. Which platforms must the first playable version support: Android, Google TV, Roku?
Q27. What exact devices will be used for the first playtest?
Q28. Must installed matches work without internet?
Q29. Same-house play only, or remote participants too?
Q30. Does every player have a personal device, or must TV-only players be supported?
Q31. Can TV deck creation be visible to the room, or must it remain private?
Q32. What reading/input assistance is wanted: spoken cards, large text, explanations, undo, remote navigation?
Q33. Where may a match run: TV, phone or home server? Explain implications after verifying platform feasibility.
Q34. What should happen after disconnection or battery failure, and should matches pause/save/resume?
Q35. Where should profiles/decks be available, and how should joining and hand privacy work?
Q36. Who may manage profiles, approve content, alter rules and delete decks?
Q37. How should installation and updates work?
Q38. What budget and ongoing costs are acceptable? Clarify content/art tools and services only if relevant.
Q39. What minimum prototype would feel like this game and merit continuing?
